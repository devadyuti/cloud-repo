Solid-state batteries: the breakthrough that could reshape the EV market

By Jonathan Pryce · Senior Technology Correspondent · 30 June 2026

Manufacturers across Europe and Asia are racing to commercialise solid-state battery cells, a technology that promises significantly greater driving range, far faster charging times and improved safety compared with the lithium-ion packs that power today's electric vehicles. By replacing the liquid electrolyte with a solid material, engineers can pack more energy into a smaller, lighter cell while reducing the risk of overheating.

Early pilot production lines suggest energy densities well beyond current chemistries, with some prototypes targeting more than 500 kilometres of range on a single charge. The defining challenge over the next two years is no longer whether the technology works in the laboratory, but whether it can be manufactured at scale and at a cost that makes mass-market vehicles viable.

Analysts caution that supply-chain readiness will ultimately decide which carmakers reach high volumes first. Securing reliable sources of raw materials, building new gigafactory capacity and proving long-term durability in real-world conditions all remain open questions. The winners, they argue, will be those who treat manufacturing as seriously as they treat the underlying chemistry.
